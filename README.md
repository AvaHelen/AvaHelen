## 👋 About Me

I am reading for an **MPhil in Linguistics and Language Science** at the [University of Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/) (2026–2027, expected), after completing a **B.A. in English** at [Northeastern University](https://www.neu.edu.cn/), Shenyang (2022–2026, GPA 92.68/100, 4.27/5.0).

I work on **second language acquisition, pragmatics and corpus linguistics**. Two threads run through my research so far: how Chinese EFL learners' affect vocabulary is shaped by cross-linguistic conceptual transfer — studied with an AI-assisted contrastive corpus of learner and native writing — and how the linguistic resources of AI-generated dialogue, such as discourse markers, change whether L2 learners perceive an interlocutor as caring. Both strands come down to the same question: what happens to meaning — emotional, pragmatic, interpersonal — when it crosses a language boundary or a machine.

**🔍 Research Interests:** Second Language Acquisition & Bilingualism · Instructed SLA · Pragmatics · Lexical & Syntactic Processing · Cross-linguistic Influence · Corpus Linguistics

## 📝 Publications

**Impact of Discourse Markers on L2 Learners' Perception of Empathy in AI-Mediated Dialogues**
*Zhang, H.* · **ICLCC 2026** — 4th International Conference on Language and Cultural Communication · Beijing, China · April 24–26, 2026 *accepted for presentation*
> A within-subject experiment in which AI responses were manipulated through prompt engineering to vary the presence of discourse markers, plus an empathy-perception questionnaire adapted from Davis' Interpersonal Reactivity Index (IRI) and aligned with linguistic-pragmatic variables. Data from 23 Chinese EFL learners, analysed with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and Spearman correlations. AI responses containing discourse markers were rated significantly higher on **perceived care, naturalness, understanding and perspective-taking**, with the effect strongest among learners of higher overall English proficiency.

## 🔬 Research Projects

**Cross-linguistic Conceptual Transfer of Affective Words in Chinese EFL Learners' Writing: An AI-Assisted Contrastive Corpus Study** · *Undergraduate Dissertation* · Jan. – Jul. 2026
Investigated conceptual transfer in learners' use of **emotion-label** and **emotion-laden** words, focusing on lexical frequency, diversity, collocation and proficiency-related variation. Built an analytical framework combining Jarvis & Pavlenko's conceptual transfer theory with the Valence–Arousal–Dominance (VAD) model, and constructed a contrastive dataset of **400 argumentative essays from ICNALE** (300 Chinese EFL learner essays, 100 native English essays) across two writing topics and three proficiency levels. Analyses in AntConc, Python and Excel, with AI-assisted conceptual reconstruction, manual annotation and statistical testing in SPSS. **Finding:** learners used emotion-laden words more frequently but with lower lexical diversity, and lower-proficiency learners showed a higher frequency of potential transfer.

**The Impact of Discourse Markers on L2 Learners' Perception of Empathy in AI Dialogues** · *Independent Research Project* · Mar. – Aug. 2025
Designed a within-subject experiment and manipulated AI responses via prompt engineering to create matched conditions with and without discourse markers; designed and validated an empathy-perception questionnaire adapted from the IRI. Analysed data from 23 Chinese EFL learners with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and Spearman correlations in SPSS.

**A Multi-modal Analysis of Female Identity Construction in Xiaohongshu Digital Advertising** · *Project Leader*, College Students' Innovation and Entrepreneurship Project · Dec. 2023 – Mar. 2025 · Supervisor: Prof. Lixin Gao
Built the research framework on multimodal discourse theory (Gunther Kress & Theo van Leeuwen) and Russell's circumplex model of emotion; led the team in questionnaire design, data processing and SPSS analysis; identified the specific female identities communicated through multimodal resources and examined correlations between advertisements' textual and visual elements and identity construction.

**Effectiveness Analysis of Cognitive Psychology-based Language Communication Seminar** · *Participant* · Jul. – Dec. 2023 · Lecturer & Supervisor: Dr. Rosemary J. Harris (University College London)
Studied phonetic and phonological theory through extensive literature work and compared the phonological systems of English and Spanish.
