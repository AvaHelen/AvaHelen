01About Me

I am reading for an MPhil in Linguistics and Language Science at the University of Cambridge (2026–2027, expected), after completing a B.A. in English at Northeastern University, Shenyang (2022–2026, GPA 92.68/100, 4.27/5.0).

I work on second language acquisition, pragmatics and corpus linguistics. Two threads run through my research so far: how Chinese EFL learners' affect vocabulary is shaped by cross-linguistic conceptual transfer — studied with an AI-assisted contrastive corpus of learner and native writing — and how the linguistic resources of AI-generated dialogue, such as discourse markers, change whether L2 learners perceive an interlocutor as caring. Both strands come down to the same question: what happens to meaning — emotional, pragmatic, interpersonal — when it crosses a language boundary or a machine.

03Publications

ICLCC 2026 · Accepted

Impact of Discourse Markers on L2 Learners' Perception of Empathy in AI-Mediated Dialogues

Hanwen Zhang

4th International Conference on Language and Cultural Communication · Beijing, China · April 24–26, 2026

Abstract

A within-subject experiment in which AI responses were manipulated through prompt engineering to vary the presence of discourse markers, combined with an empathy-perception questionnaire adapted from Davis' Interpersonal Reactivity Index (IRI) and aligned with linguistic-pragmatic variables. Data from 23 Chinese EFL learners were analysed with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and Spearman correlations. AI responses containing discourse markers were rated significantly higher on perceived care, naturalness, understanding and perspective-taking, with the effect strongest among learners of higher overall English proficiency.

04Research Projects

Cross-linguistic Conceptual Transfer of Affective Words in Chinese EFL Learners' WritingUndergraduate Dissertation

Jan – Jul 2026

AI-assisted contrastive corpus study

  • Investigated conceptual transfer in learners' use of emotion-label and emotion-laden words, focusing on lexical frequency, diversity, collocation and proficiency-related variation.
  • Built an analytical framework combining Jarvis & Pavlenko's conceptual transfer theory with the Valence–Arousal–Dominance (VAD) model.
  • Constructed a contrastive dataset of 400 argumentative essays from ICNALE — 300 Chinese EFL learner essays and 100 native English essays — across two writing topics and three proficiency levels.
  • AntConc, Python and Excel for analysis; AI-assisted conceptual reconstruction and manual annotation; statistical testing in SPSS.
  • Finding: learners used emotion-laden words more frequently but with lower lexical diversity, and lower-proficiency learners showed a higher frequency of potential transfer.
